Environmental justice

Pollution and heat don't fall evenly. Some neighborhoods carry far more of the burden.

What is an environmental justice neighborhood?

Massachusetts law defines an environmental justice population as a neighborhood meeting at least one of these:

  • Median household income is 65% or less of the state median
  • People of color make up 40% or more of residents
  • 25% or more of households lack English proficiency
  • People of color make up 25% or more, and the town's income is 150% or less of the state median

Why it matters

  • Chinatown sits where Interstates 90 and 93 meet. An EPA study found it has the highest fine-particle levels of any neighborhood in Massachusetts.
  • Emergency room visits for asthma are highest in Mattapan, followed by Roxbury and Dorchester.
  • Boston's hottest days are getting more common. The city averaged about 10 days a year over 90°F; by the 2070s that could reach about 60 if emissions continue at the current rate.

Official estimate vs. measured from the air

Researchers measuring methane in Boston's air found emissions from the gas system about 6 times higher than the state's official estimate. Independent monitoring like our station helps check the official numbers.
